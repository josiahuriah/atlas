Bringing your dog or cat to The Bahamas is very doable — but it isn't something you can sort out at the airport. Every pet arriving in the country must have an approved import permit before it lands, and the paperwork behind that permit has to meet specific veterinary standards.

This guide walks you through what's required, how long each step takes, and how to avoid the most common reason applications get sent back.

<PullQuote>Please note: this guide covers **dogs and cats only**. If you're bringing in any other animal — birds, reptiles, rabbits, fish or otherwise — please [contact us directly](/contact) so we can advise on the procedures that apply.</PullQuote>

## What Every Pet Owner Needs: A BAHFSA Pet Permit

Anyone bringing a pet into The Bahamas must hold a pet permit issued by the **[Bahamas Agricultural Health and Food Safety Authority (BAHFSA)](https://bahfsabahamas.com/)**. BAHFSA reviews your pet's health documentation and, once satisfied, approves the permit that allows your pet to enter the country.

Your pet's entry is also processed through the Bahamas Customs Click2Clear system. When you use Atlas Brokerage, we take care of your Click2Clear registration as part of our brokerage service — there's nothing extra for you to do.

## How Long Does It Take?

Timing is where most people get caught out. A standard BAHFSA pet permit takes **3 business days** to be approved, and your Click2Clear registration (which we handle for you) takes **1 business day**. Weekends and public holidays don't count — so a request made on a Thursday afternoon won't be approved until the following week.

For that reason, **we recommend beginning the application process at least one week before you travel.** That buffer gives you time to fix anything BAHFSA queries without putting your travel plans at risk.

**What counts as an official vaccination record?**

This is the document that causes the most delays, so it's worth getting right the first time. Your vaccination record must be an official record from your veterinarian that clearly shows:

- **The administering clinic and veterinarian**
- **Your pet's details** — name, age, gender, breed and microchip number
- **The vaccinations and treatments received**, and **how long each one is valid for**

<PullQuote>Vaccination receipts are not accepted. An invoice or payment receipt from your vet — even one that lists the vaccines — is not a vaccination record. Ask your clinic for the official record.</PullQuote>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I get a pet permit when I land in The Bahamas?**

No. Your pet permit must be approved before your pet arrives. Without one, your pet may be refused entry.

**My vet gave me a receipt that lists the vaccines. Is that enough?**

No. BAHFSA requires an official vaccination record showing the clinic, the veterinarian, your pet's full details including microchip number, and the validity period of each vaccine. Receipts are not accepted.

**My pet's vaccines expire while I'm in The Bahamas. Is that a problem?**

It can be. Your pet's vaccinations should remain valid up to the date you plan to leave The Bahamas. If a vaccine is due to expire during your stay, speak with your vet about a booster before you travel.
